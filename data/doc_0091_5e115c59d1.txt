The session on interprofessional collaboration in sexual, reproductive and maternal health highlighted the critical role of effective teamwork, particularly between midwives and obstetricians, in improving quality, safety, equity and efficiency of care across the maternal and reproductive health continuum. Presentations from WHO, International Confederation of Midwives (ICM) and United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) emphasized that while role clarity and competency mapping are essential foundations, true collaboration also requires shared leadership, trust, communication, supportive regulation and cultural change within health systems. The session on the future of the Global Strategy for Women’s, Children’s and Adolescents’ Health underscored the need to reassess existing frameworks in light of significant global megatrends, including demographic change, climate risks, geopolitical fragmentation, constrained financing and rapid technological advances. Participants reaffirmed WHO’s leadership, with the United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) and the Partnership for Maternal, Newborn & Child Health (PMNCH) co-leading a renewed, equity-centred process grounded in country realities and informed by lessons from the 2010 and 2015 strategies. STAGE emphasized a life-course approach that fully integrates women’s health, early and inclusive engagement of diverse stakeholders, and prioritization of a small number of high-impact background documents, particularly evaluations of past strategies and assessments of global megatrends. WHO confirmed a phased, focused technical approach and suggested to return to STAGE with next steps in 2026. The opening session of the 12th meeting of the Strategic and Technical Advisory Group of Experts (STAGE) highlighted the importance of convening amid funding constraints, humanitarian crises, and the major transition within the World Health Organization (WHO) and the broader global health architecture. Held virtually, the session outlined the two-day agenda, noted the conclusion of STAGE’s current funding cycle in mid-2026, and emphasized a strengthened role for STAGE in providing independent technical advice. Dr Pascale Allotey, Director, Sexual, Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child, and Adolescent Health and Ageing: Advancing Life Course Health and Reproduction (LHR), presented organization- wide restructuring and prioritization in response to budget reductions. The restructure involved consolidation of structures, adoption of a life- course approach through the new LHR department, and a sharper focus on normative guidance, measurable outcomes and accountability. Dr Luz De Regil, Director, Nutrition and Food Safety (NFS) provided updates on nutrition and food safety and emphasized streamlined priorities and strengthened partnerships. Discussion underscored the need to safeguard equity, support vulnerable countries, maintain SDG alignment, and strengthen inter-agency collaboration. A proposed management review will systematically examine the advisory architecture, including STAGE, to assess mandates, performance, coherence, and value for money, and to identify options and a roadmap for advisory arrangements beyond the current funding period. In parallel, findings from a rapid internal survey confirmed that STAGE is valued for its independent and credible advice but highlighted the need for a clearer, more strategic mandate, stronger prioritization and better linkage between recommendations and country-level impact. [ ] vii Background All STAGE members attending the meeting provided their declaration of interest. Eleven members had identified conflicts, mainly related to grants received by their institutions for doing research in their area of expertise. None of the reported conflicts were perceived to have any impact on the members’ ability to join the meeting or to provide an objective and impartial contribution to any of the sessions. WHO, through its departments of Sexual, Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child, and Adolescent Health and Ageing: Advancing Life Course Health and Reproduction (LHR) and Nutrition and Food Safety (NFS), organized the 12th meeting of the Strategic and Technical Advisory Group of Experts (STAGE) on 18–19 November 2025. This online meeting with a two- day agenda (Annex 1) was attended by 28 STAGE members, WHO staff from headquarters and the regions, and observers from partner and other organizations (Annex 2). The two-day (3 hours per day) meeting agenda comprised an update on the status, direction and vision of the LHR and NFS departments that were part of the restructure at WHO; interprofessional collaborations in sexual, reproductive and maternal health; and the future of the Global Strategy for Women’s, Children’s and Adolescents’ Health. In addition, as part of discussions on the future of STAGE as an advisory body, evaluation plans for all advisory groups within LHR and results from a recent survey of STAGE were presented. The preparatory work included background documents for all sessions, along with a detailed feedback report on activities undertaken by the WHO team in response to previous STAGE recommendations. In addition, smaller group meetings with a few STAGE members were held to review and discuss the topics for presentation to STAGE. For the technical sessions, WHO technical teams provided detailed presentations, while STAGE members supported with draft guidance or next steps. This was followed by discussions first by STAGE members and then by WHO regional offices providing a country/regional perspective. Partners and other observers also provided their inputs. WHO clarified that equity remained central to prioritization, that headquarters would retain responsibility for global norms and selected strategic initiatives while regional and country offices would lead implementation, that SDG alignment remained embedded within WHO’s results framework, and that renewed mechanisms for collaboration and inter-agency coordination were being strengthened. Professor Caroline Homer, Chair of STAGE, opened the 12th STAGE meeting by welcoming STAGE members and partners from WHO headquarters and regions, UN partners, professional organizations, donors and other partners. She noted that the meeting was being held virtually due to the current global context of funding contraction, conflict and humanitarian crises, and she underscored the significance of convening at a moment of major transition within WHO and the broader global health architecture. She highlighted that STAGE’s funding cycle would conclude in mid- 2026 and outlined the two-day agenda, which included an overview of WHO’s restructuring, a review of recent STAGE recommendations, discussions on interprofessional collaboration and the Global Strategy for Women’s, Children’s and Adolescents’ Health, and reflection on the future value proposition of STAGE. She emphasized that STAGE members would play a more active role as lead discussants to ensure recommendations remained firmly grounded in independent technical advice. Dr Pascale Allotey, Director of WHO’s Department of Sexual, Reproductive, Maternal, Child, Adolescent Health and Ageing (LHR), presented an overview of WHO’s organization-wide prioritization and realignment process, undertaken in response to a long-standing reliance on voluntary financing and the requirement to immediately reduce the budget by at least 21%. She outlined that WHO had adopted a strategic, values-based approach, guided by transparency, fairness and humanity, rather than uniform budget cuts, resulting in a consolidation from 11 divisions to six and from 67 departments to 36. She described the creation of the LHR department through the integration of Maternal, Newborn, Child and Adolescent Health and Ageing (MCA), Sexual and Reproductive Health and Research (SRH) and the Human Reproduction Programme (HRP) under a unified life-course framework. She noted growing momentum across WHO, UNFPA, ICM and International Federation of Obstetrics and Gynaecology (FIGO) to strengthen collaboration between midwives and obstetricians, including recent discussions at the FIGO Congress, and framed the session as an opportunity for STAGE to provide strategic guidance on advancing this agenda at country level. Dr Ulrika Rehnström Loi (WHO) then introduced the conceptual foundations of interprofessional collaboration, reiterating that evidence consistently demonstrates that collaboration between midwives and obstetricians enhances safety, reduces unnecessary interventions, strengthens referral pathways, improves women’s experiences of care, and contributes to more efficient workforce utilization. Drawing on WHO’s historical work and the transition framework within the midwifery models of care implementation guidance, she explained that full collaboration requires equal partnership, shared leadership, role clarity and integrated professional education, with Phase 5 of the framework representing the goal of fully embedded collaboration across health systems. She emphasized that role clarity, built into pre-service and in-service training and reinforced through regulation and national policy, is a core condition for achieving this objective. She noted that while WHO provides detailed competency mappings for abortion care, no equivalent evidence-based mapping yet exists for maternal health, thereby underscoring the need for further joint work. She also highlighted the joint initiative with ICM and [ ] 6 Report of the twelfth meeting of the WHO Strategic and Technical Advisory Group of Experts for Maternal, Newborn, Child and Adolescent Health and Nutrition, 18–19 November 2025 mentorship, shared curricula, and collaborative training of obstetric and midwifery associations, as essential to shifting entrenched dynamics. Examples from Bangladesh, Chile, the Dominican Republic, Kenya, South Sudan and Venezuela (Bolivarian Republic of) illustrated how engaging national obstetrics and gynaecology professional associations as implementing partners can accelerate acceptance of midwives’ competencies and promote evidence-based role delineation. She emphasized that interprofessional collaboration is “non-optional and non-negotiable” for achieving SRMNAH goals, and she called for strengthened regulation, clearly defined scopes of practice, and deliberate efforts to improve mutual respect across professional groups. Frank Louwen (FIGO), who could not attend this session, provided comments later and welcomed the contributions to the forthcoming FIGO–ICM joint statements. The session moved to discussion following Professor Homer’s invitation for STAGE to respond to guiding questions concerning how WHO and partners could support Member States to embed interprofessional collaboration into national policy, regulation and practice. Discussion STAGE members broadly welcomed the initiative and acknowledged the compelling rationale for stronger collaboration, while raising several substantive considerations. Members first sought greater clarity on the scope of “interprofessional collaboration”, observing that the presentations focused heavily on delineating what midwives and obstetricians can each safely do, rather than the broader relational, team-based dimensions necessary for effective collaboration. They emphasized that collaboration must encompass communication, shared decision- making and cultural transformation within teams, and that role clarity alone is insufficient without parallel attention to trust-building and relational dynamics. Several members also noted that limiting the focus to midwives and obstetricians risks overlooking essential cadres, particularly nurses and paediatricians, whose involvement is crucial given countries’ diverse workforce models and the continuum of care from pregnancy through early infancy. They cautioned that in many settings, midwives work alongside general physicians rather than obstetricians, and that collaboration frameworks and competency mappings should account for this reality. Members further highlighted persistent barriers such as hierarchical norms, restrictive regulation, lack of autonomy for midwives, and differing interpretations of scope of practice across countries. They questioned how WHO and partners could harmonize scopes of practice, reduce contradictory regulatory requirements, and provide countries with tools, such as minimum standards for interprofessional collaboration, to support systemic reform. Economic considerations were also raised, including the value of demonstrating the financial benefits of collaborative practice and aligning payment models with team-based care. Members asked for clearer articulation of the specific life course period addressed, noting confusion between examples focused on family planning and comprehensive abortion care, and broader reproductive and maternal health. Partners contributed to the discussion, with Professor Jacqueline Dunkley-Bent (ICM) underscoring that the joint ICM–FIGO statements aim to reset professional narratives by centring service users and establishing midwives and obstetricians as equal but distinct contributors. She highlighted that while the initiative begins with these two professions, it is understood as iterative and designed to expand to other cadres, and that its current scope reflects the practical starting point of collaboration between ICM and FIGO. In their responses, WHO and partners clarified that interprofessional collaboration is intended to span the reproductive and maternal health continuum, not solely abortion or intrapartum care, and that the examples presented reflect ongoing work rather than the full scope of the intended initiative. Ulrika Rehnström Loi reiterated that the focus on roles and responsibilities was deliberately framed as a “first step”, mirroring the approach taken in WHO’s abortion care guidelines where evidence- based competency mappings have supported policy reform. Allisyn Moran added that the initiative is envisioned as a pathway towards inclusion of additional professions, but that establishing [ ] Technical sessions 7 clearer shared understanding between midwives and obstetricians is foundational for broader expansion. WHO also acknowledged the need to address regulatory barriers, strengthen relational components of teamwork, and ensure that future work includes other cadres central to delivering person-centred, high-quality care. The session concluded with agreement that STAGE members would refine formal recommendations for WHO based on the guiding questions, and that future work should balance the need for a clear entry point with a commitment to broader, system- wide interprofessional collaboration. STAGE guidance Professor Jane Sandall (STAGE) explained that the guidance is intended to focus specifically on pregnancy, intrapartum and early postpartum maternal and newborn care, and to be applicable across both high- and low-income settings. She emphasized that the intent is not to define individual practitioner competencies, but rather to address areas of overlap in scope of practice at the occupational level among relevant health professionals. This proposal builds on STAGE’s prior work on midwifery models of care and responds directly to recurring questions raised by professional associations, countries and regions during consultations and webinars. STAGE therefore agreed to recommend that WHO develop evidence-based guidance to provide role clarity around specific components of maternal and newborn care across the continuum. Such guidance should be informed by global evidence and best practices, offer clear direction on the allocation of responsibilities across antenatal, intrapartum and postpartum services, and aim to optimize the available health workforce while ensuring safe and effective care and improved outcomes for women and newborn. Members further underscored that this guidance should serve as a foundation for continued dialogue on interprofessional collaboration, supporting trust and partnership among stakeholders. He noted that these frameworks, alongside the Comprehensive Implementation Plan on Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition, have guided global action through the Millennium Development Goal and Sustainable Development Goal eras but are increasingly misaligned with current and emerging global realities.
